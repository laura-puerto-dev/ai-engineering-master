## 1. Purpose

Northfern Technologies protects company, employee, partner and customer information throughout its operations. This policy sets the minimum information-security requirements for all employees, regardless of their country, department or work location. Local rules and applicable law may impose additional requirements.

## 2. Information classification

Information must be handled according to its classification and the recipient's legitimate need to know:

| Classification | Description | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Approved for external distribution | Published marketing materials |
| Internal | Intended for authorized Northfern personnel | General internal policies and operational guidance |
| Confidential | Sensitive business or personal information with limited authorized access | Customer contracts, non-public commercial information, employee administrative records |
| Restricted | Highly sensitive information requiring explicitly limited access | Credentials, security secrets, privileged investigation records |

Classification does not grant access by itself. Employees may access information only when their role and responsibilities authorize it. Documents must not be downgraded or shared more broadly merely for convenience.

## 3. Accounts, authentication and access

Corporate accounts are individual and must not be shared. Passwords, authentication tokens and recovery codes must never be disclosed to colleagues or third parties. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is mandatory wherever supported by corporate services.

Access is granted on a least-privilege, need-to-know basis. Employees must not attempt to retrieve records or documents outside their authorization, even if a system appears to permit it. Suspected excessive access must be reported to Information Security.

## 4. Corporate devices and approved services

Employees must use Northfern-managed and authorized devices for corporate work and access to internal systems. Personally owned devices must not be used to access corporate applications or data unless a separately documented exception has been approved in advance by Information Security.

Company information must be stored and exchanged only through approved repositories, collaboration platforms and communication channels. Employees must not move work files to personal cloud storage or personal email accounts.

## 5. Sharing and transferring information

Before sharing information, employees must verify its classification, the recipient's identity, the business purpose and the authorized channel. Confidential and Restricted information must not be shared with unauthorized recipients, including other Northfern employees without a legitimate need to know.

Cross-border access or transfer of information may require additional review. Being on an approved device or using MFA does not automatically authorize access from another country or transfer of data across borders.

## 6. Use of artificial intelligence tools

Only AI tools and services explicitly approved by Northfern may be used for company work. Approval of one tool does not imply approval of other tools from the same provider.

Employees must not enter Confidential or Restricted information into an unapproved AI service. They must also avoid entering Internal information into unapproved services unless Information Security has expressly authorized that use. Public information may be used only in a manner consistent with the tool's approved business purpose and other applicable company policies.

AI-generated output must be reviewed before use or distribution. Employees remain responsible for verifying accuracy, confidentiality and appropriate handling of any source material.

For example, an employee must not paste a customer contract into a public AI chatbot merely to obtain a summary. They must first verify the contract's classification and whether an approved tool and workflow exist.

## 7. Remote work and travel

Security requirements apply equally in offices, at home and during business travel. Employees must use approved devices, supported authentication methods and authorized connections. They must prevent unauthorized viewing of screens, documents and conversations in shared or public spaces.

Employees traveling internationally must follow destination-specific security instructions issued by Information Security. Compliance with this policy does **not** constitute permission to work from another country. Employees based in Spain must also comply with **ES-003 — Teletrabajo y flexibilidad horaria**, including its requirement for specific prior authorization for work abroad. Equivalent local rules apply to employees in other countries.

## 8. Security incidents and lost devices

Employees must report suspected security incidents **immediately upon discovery** through the designated internal security-reporting channel. This includes suspected phishing, accidental disclosure, unauthorized access, lost credentials and unusual account activity.

Loss or theft of a corporate device must be reported immediately, even if the employee has no evidence that company data was accessed. Employees should provide factual details and follow instructions from Information Security; they must not conceal incidents or conduct unauthorized investigations.

## 9. Exceptions

Any exception to these requirements requires explicit approval from Information Security **before** the exception is used. A manager's operational approval does not replace a required security approval. Approved exceptions must be documented through the designated corporate process, with their scope and duration defined.

An exception to a security control does not automatically waive employment, privacy, travel or local policy requirements.

## 10. Responsibilities

All employees must protect the information they use, follow approved processes and report incidents promptly. Managers must reinforce these requirements within their teams and refer requests outside their authority to the appropriate function. Information Security owns security-control decisions and coordinates incident response. Human Resources and other departments remain responsible for decisions within their respective areas.

## 11. Related policies

- **GLB-001 — Code of Conduct:** professional integrity, confidentiality and responsible use of corporate resources and AI.
- **ES-003 — Teletrabajo y flexibilidad horaria:** remote-work eligibility and authorization for employees based in Spain.
- **ES-005 — Viajes y gastos profesionales:** business-travel approval and reimbursement for employees based in Spain.
- **ES-007 — Procedimientos internos de Recursos Humanos:** HR-only handling and escalation of exceptional cases in Spain. This reference does not grant access to that restricted document.

Where a local policy introduces additional controls, employees must follow both the global security requirements and the applicable local rules. Questions about a specific authorization must be referred to the relevant responsible function rather than inferred from this policy alone.

## 12. Compliance and review

Failure to follow this policy may result in review under applicable company procedures and local law. Global Information Security reviews this policy periodically and communicates material changes through approved internal channels.
